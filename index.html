01 / Language AWAM: legal questions need sources, not confidence Accessible Wisdom for Law & Advocacy Matters

Risk A legal answer can sound useful while resting on a source the person reading it cannot inspect.

Decision AWAM maps everyday questions into legal language, retrieves cited material, and leaves the final judgment outside the model. I am rebuilding the corpus around official and permission-reviewed materials before reopening it.

02 / Judgment When two LLM judges disagreed A score is only useful when it survives being repeated.

Risk Two LLM judges produced convincing essay scores, but repeating the judgment exposed different stability, bias, and review workload.

Decision I tested 150 essays with two models across five runs. GPT was more consistent; Qwen scored 74 of 95 matched essays higher and sent 92.2% to human review. Do not mix models within one cohort.

03 / Vision Know Your Sight: accuracy was not the final review 90.64% looked good. Glaucoma recall told us where it was not enough.

Risk Overall accuracy reached 90.64%, but that average hid glaucoma recall at 80.33%.

Decision I fine-tuned EfficientNetB0, reviewed precision and recall per class, then integrated it into Jaya Koding's web prototype. The work earned competition placements, not clinical validation.
